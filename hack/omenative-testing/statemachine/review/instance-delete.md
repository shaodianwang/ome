# delete — arrows and exceptions

## ARROWS (every outgoing transition of Delete/Drain)

| id | from → to | on | guard (as written) | verdict | change / question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-delete-done | Delete/Drain → Empty | pod.deleted[source] | every pod of the index is gone and its instance resources are finalized, with the wave's completion guard still matching | CHANGE (widen the event set) | Completion is also reached with no pod event at all: a podless candidate completes on `FinalizeInstanceResources` returning complete (`delete_batch.go:382-393`) and an index whose last pod vanished before the pass completes on a bare resync — so `ctrl.resync` and `gang.podGroup[Terminating]` must be triggering events of this arrow, not `ignore`/`defer` in every window (EX-5, EX-6). |
| T-delete-force | Delete/Drain → Delete/Drain | pod.stuckTerminating | a force-delete policy is configured, the pod is past its grace plus slack, carries no foreign finalizer, and its node is gone or has been unreachable longer than the threshold | CHANGE (merge with T-delete-force-clock) | The guard itself is right — a `Ready=True` node means the kubelet is alive and forcing risks a double-running container (`force_delete.go:182-196`). But this arrow and T-delete-force-clock are one call (`escalateStuckTerminatingWithDeadline`) classified once per pod per pass; splitting them by which event "names" it buys four cross-cells that all say "the other arrow did it". Merge into one arrow with two triggering events. Separately: this is the *only* liveness escape from the state, and it cannot fire on a healthy node (EX-1). |
| T-delete-force-clock | Delete/Drain → Delete/Drain | timer.forceDelete | a force-delete policy is configured and the pod is past its deletion deadline plus the overdue slack while its node is gone or has been unreachable past the threshold; both clocks are required and whichever lands last is the trigger, and the wake is scheduled for exactly that boundary | CHANGE (guard text + merge) | "the wake is scheduled for exactly that boundary" is false on two branches: `evidenceNodeHealthy` wakes at `now + NodeUnreachableThreshold` (`force_delete.go:192, :244`) and a nil-`TimeAdded` taint contributes no boundary at all — both are live-recheck cadences, which is what the sibling cell claims the code refuses to invent (EX-14). Merge as above. |
| T-delete-teardown-deadline | Delete/Drain → Empty | timer.teardownDeadline | the owner is Terminating, the configured teardown deadline has passed since its deletionTimestamp, and the teardown finalizer is still held | ASK (lean CHANGE the effects text; keep the release) | Effects say "surviving pods and their owner references left to background GC", but a pod already wedged Terminating is exactly the pod GC cannot collect — this arrow *strands* it and simultaneously stops the designed unwedger (`teardown.go:96-113` runs before the workload dispatch). Two questions for the owner: (a) should the effects state plainly that pods already Terminating survive the release; (b) the deadline is measured from the owner's deletionTimestamp, not from lack of progress, so a legitimately long `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` converts an orderly drain into a GC dump — should the release be suppressed while the wave is demonstrably making progress? Lean: fix (a) now, ASK (b). |
